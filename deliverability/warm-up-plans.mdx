## Why warmup matters

- **Reputation starts at zero.** Mailbox providers judge a new IP by its first sending patterns. Sudden high volume looks like a spammer or a compromised server.
- **Providers throttle unknown senders.** Without warmup, much of your mail is deferred or rejected (4xx/5xx responses) before reaching the inbox.
- **Early damage is hard to undo.** A blocklisting or poor reputation in week one can take weeks to repair.
- **Good habits stick.** A steady ramp with engaged recipients gives your IP a strong reputation that carries your full volume long term.

## Domain requirements

Before you start, make sure your domain:

| Requirement | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Domain history | 6+ months of real sending history |
| SPF | Published and includes mySMTP. See [SPF, DKIM, DMARC & BIMI](/deliverability/spf-dkim-dmarc-and-bimi-explained) |
| DKIM | Signing active for your domain |
| DMARC | Published, starting with `p=none` |
| Blocklists | Domain is not listed. See [Blacklist monitoring](/deliverability/blacklist-monitoring-and-delisting) |

## Build your schedule with the generator

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the generator">
    Go to [toolbox.smtp.ai/#warmup-generator](https://toolbox.smtp.ai/#warmup-generator).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your details">
    | Input | What to enter |
    | --- | --- |
    | Server / IP | `relayxx.mysmtp.com` (from your mySMTP setup email) |
    | Email Type | Marketing, Transactional, Mixed, or Cold Outreach |
    | Target Daily Volume | The daily volume you want to reach |
    | Warmup Speed | Conservative, Moderate, or Aggressive |
    | Starting Volume | Emails on day 1 (default 50) |
    | Domain Reputation | Your domain's sending history and reputation |
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a warmup speed">
    | Speed | Duration | Best for |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Conservative | 45 days | Cold outreach, or when inbox placement is critical |
    | Moderate | 30 days | Most senders with clean lists |
    | Aggressive | 21 days | Domains with a strong reputation and high engagement |
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate and export">
    The generator shows overview cards, a daily volume chart, a day-by-day table (target volume, increase, and progress), and recommendations for your email type. Export the schedule as CSV to track it in a spreadsheet or your sending platform.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send every day">
    Follow the schedule daily, starting with your most engaged recipients. Don't skip days or jump ahead.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  The schedule grows exponentially for roughly the first 70% of the days, then linearly up to your target. This mirrors how a real email program grows, which providers recognize as legitimate behavior.
</Note>

## Metrics to watch

Check these daily. If a metric hits the warning level, pause or slow down.

| Metric | Healthy | Warning | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bounce rate | Under 2% | Over 5% | Pause and clean your list |
| Spam complaint rate | Under 0.1% | Over 0.3% | Pause immediately |
| Inbox placement | Over 95% | Under 70% | Slow down the ramp |
| Open rate | Over 20% | Under 10% | Review content and list quality |

<Tip>
  Track Gmail reputation in [Google Postmaster Tools](https://postmaster.google.com/) and Microsoft reputation in [SNDS](https://sendersupport.olc.protection.outlook.com/snds/).
</Tip>

## Common mistakes

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Using a new or recently registered domain">
    A domain with less than 6 months of history has no reputation to carry. Use an established domain with at least 6 months of sending history. Do not use a new domain for warmup.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Skipping days">
    Gaps create irregular volume patterns. Send every day during warmup, even small amounts.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Increasing volume too fast">
    Big jumps trigger filters and throttling. Follow the generated schedule.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Sending to your full list right away">
    Start with recent, engaged recipients and add less engaged segments later.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ignoring warning signs">
    High complaints, rising bounces, or low opens mean you should pause, not push through.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Re-warming after a pause

| Inactive period | How to resume |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 2 weeks | Resume at 50% of previous volume, ramp back over 1 week |
| 1 to 2 months | Resume at 25% of volume, follow a 2-week ramp |
| 3+ months | Treat as a new IP and run the full warmup |

If you warm up several IPs, stagger the start dates by 1 to 2 weeks and generate a separate schedule for each IP.
