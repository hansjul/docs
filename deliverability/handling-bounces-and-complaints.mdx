## Bounce types

| Type | Meaning | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hard bounce | Permanent failure. The address does not exist or the domain is invalid. | Remove immediately. |
| Soft bounce | Temporary failure. Mailbox full, server down, or message too large. | Retry a few times, then treat as hard bounce if delivery keeps failing. |

### Common hard bounce causes

- The recipient email address does not exist
- The domain name is misspelled or no longer active
- The recipient server has blocked delivery permanently

### Common soft bounce causes

- The recipient mailbox is over quota
- The receiving mail server is temporarily unavailable
- The message exceeds the recipient size limit

## Suppression lists

mySMTP maintains suppression lists to prevent future sends to addresses that have already bounced, complained, or unsubscribed. This improves deliverability and compliance automatically.

### Automatic suppression

MailWizz adds an address to the suppression list when any of these events occur:

- A hard bounce is received
- A recipient submits a spam complaint via a feedback loop (FBL)
- A recipient unsubscribes

### Manual suppression

You can also add addresses manually to prevent sending to contacts you no longer want to reach.

## Handling complaints

A complaint happens when a recipient clicks the spam or junk button in their email client. mySMTP integrates with feedback loops from major providers to detect these events and suppress the address automatically.

### How to reduce complaints

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use confirmed opt-in">
    Only send to recipients who have explicitly opted in. Confirmed (double) opt-in is the safest method.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use a recognizable sender">
    Use a from name and from address that recipients will recognize.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make unsubscribing easy">
    Include a visible, one-click unsubscribe link in every marketing message. Honor opt-out requests immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send relevant content">
    Segment your lists and send targeted content. Avoid excessive frequency.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Spam complaint rates above 0.1% are concerning. Rates above 0.3% can cause throttling or blocks at major providers like Gmail and Yahoo.
</Warning>

## Monitoring and best practices

- Review bounce and complaint reports in your mySMTP dashboard regularly.
- Remove hard bounces immediately. mySMTP suppresses them automatically.
- Clean your lists periodically to remove inactive or invalid addresses.
- Segment by engagement and remove subscribers who have not opened or clicked in 6 to 12 months.
- Authenticate with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC to improve deliverability and reduce spoofing complaints.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Bounce rate is suddenly high">
    Pause the campaign and review the bounce reasons. Check for list import errors, stale data, or a bad domain. Remove invalid addresses before resuming.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Complaint rate is climbing">
    Reduce frequency, tighten opt-in practices, and review your from name. Make sure the unsubscribe link is visible and functional. Segment to engaged recipients only.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Soft bounces are not converting to hard bounces">
    If an address soft-bounces repeatedly over several campaigns, treat it as a hard bounce and remove it manually.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
