Moving from Postmark to mySMTP means updating your SMTP hostname, authentication method, and domain DNS records. This guide shows exactly what to change so nothing breaks during the transition.

## What changes

| Setting | Postmark (old) | mySMTP (new) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SMTP server | `smtp.postmarkapp.com` | `relayxx.mysmtp.com` (from your setup email) |
| Shared IP (DK only) | `smtp.postmarkapp.com` | `mail.mysmtp.com` |
| Port | `587` (recommended) or `465` | `587` (STARTTLS, recommended) or `465` |
| Username | Your Postmark server token | Your mySMTP SMTP username |
| Password | Your Postmark server token | Your mySMTP SMTP password |
| SPF record | `v=spf1 include:spf.mtasv.net ~all` | `v=spf1 include:spf1.mysmtp.com ~all` |
| SPF during transition |  | `v=spf1 include:spf.mtasv.net include:spf1.mysmtp.com ~all` |

<Note>
  Your real mySMTP hostname is in your setup email. Do not use `smtp.mysmtp.com`.
</Note>

<Warning>
  Postmark uses message streams (identified by a header like `X-PM-Message-Stream`). mySMTP does not use message streams. Remove or replace any Postmark-specific headers in your code when switching to mySMTP.
</Warning>

## Before you cut over

1. **Export your suppression list** from Postmark (bounces, spam complaints, unsubscribes). Do not send to those addresses from mySMTP. mySMTP does not import suppression lists automatically.
2. **Verify DNS** for mySMTP. Add `include:spf1.mysmtp.com` to your existing SPF record (for example `v=spf1 include:spf.mtasv.net include:spf1.mysmtp.com ~all`) and add mySMTP DKIM TXT/CNAME records. Keep Postmark's records in place until the transition is stable.
3. **Test connectivity** with [test.smtp.ai](https://test.smtp.ai) using your mySMTP credentials.

## Cutover steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Update application SMTP settings">
    Replace Postmark's `smtp.postmarkapp.com` with your mySMTP hostname, enter your mySMTP username and password, and use port `587` with STARTTLS.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove Postmark-specific headers">
    If your application sets `X-PM-Message-Stream` or other Postmark headers, remove or replace them. mySMTP does not require message stream headers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send test messages">
    Send a few test emails to addresses you control. Verify headers, authentication passes, and messages land in the inbox.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Monitor delivery metrics">
    Watch bounces, complaints, and inbox placement for 24 to 72 hours. Use the [Email Header Analyzer](https://toolbox.smtp.ai/) in the mySMTP Deliverability Toolbox.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Warm up your dedicated IP">
    If you are on a dedicated IP plan (default), ramp volume gradually. See [Warm up plans](/deliverability/warm-up-plans) and use the [warm-up generator](https://toolbox.smtp.ai/#warmup-generator).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove Postmark DNS records">
    Once sending is stable and your reputation metrics are good, remove `include:spf.mtasv.net` so your SPF record reads `v=spf1 include:spf1.mysmtp.com ~all`, and delete Postmark DKIM CNAME/TXT records.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="I used Postmark's HTTP API, not SMTP. What do I do?">
    mySMTP's HTTP API is coming in Q4 2026. For now, switch to SMTP by updating your integration to use the settings in the table above. See [Code examples](/email-configuration/code-examples) for libraries in PHP, Python, Node.js, .NET, and Java.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I keep Postmark as a backup during the transition?">
    Yes. Keep Postmark DNS records and credentials active while you warm up mySMTP. Only remove them after you confirm stable delivery.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need to change my from address?">
    No. You can keep the same from addresses and domains. Just make sure the domain's SPF and DKIM include mySMTP.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What if I see a spike in bounces after switching?">
    If you did not export and exclude your Postmark suppression list, you may be mailing known bad addresses. Pause sending, clean your list, and resume with a smaller volume.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
