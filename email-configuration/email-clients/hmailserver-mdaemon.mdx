You need your SMTP username, password, and relay hostname from your mySMTP setup email.

## Before you start

Have these details ready:

| Setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Smart host | Your relay hostname, e.g. `relayxx.mysmtp.com` |
| Port | **587** |
| Encryption | **STARTTLS** |
| Username | Your mySMTP SMTP username |
| Password | Your mySMTP SMTP password |

<Note>
  Use the exact relay hostname from your setup email. `relayxx.mysmtp.com` is only an example.
</Note>

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="hMailServer">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open SMTP delivery settings">
        In hMailServer Administrator, go to **Settings** > **Protocols** > **SMTP** > **Delivery of e-mail**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Set the remote host name and port">
        1. Enter your relay hostname (e.g. `relayxx.mysmtp.com`) in **Remote host name**.
        2. Enter **587** in **Remote TCP/IP port**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enable SMTP authentication">
        1. Check **Server requires authentication**.
        2. Enter your mySMTP **username** and **password**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Set connection security">
        Select **STARTTLS (Required)** from the **Connection security** dropdown. If STARTTLS is not available as an option, set it to **TLS** or the nearest equivalent and ensure the port is 587.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Save and test">
        Click **Save** and send a test email from a client connected to hMailServer.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Warning>
      Menu labels may vary slightly depending on your hMailServer version. Look for "Delivery of e-mail" or "SMTP routes" under the SMTP protocol settings.
    </Warning>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="MDaemon">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open server settings">
        In MDaemon, go to **Server** > **Server Settings**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enable the smart host">
        1. Select **Delivery**.
        2. Check **Send all mail to smart host**.
        3. Enter your relay hostname (e.g. `relayxx.mysmtp.com`) in **Smart host**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enable SMTP authentication">
        1. Check **Use SMTP AUTH**.
        2. Enter your mySMTP **username** and **password**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Set the port">
        Enter **587** as the port for the smart host connection.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Require STARTTLS">
        1. Go to **Server** > **Server Settings** > **Ports/SSL**.
        2. Make sure the smart host delivery connection uses **STARTTLS** or TLS on port 587.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Save and test">
        Click **Apply** or **OK** and send a test email from a client connected to MDaemon.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Warning>
      Menu labels may vary slightly depending on your MDaemon version. Look for "Smart host" under Delivery or Routing settings.
    </Warning>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Test the connection

1. Send a test email from a mailbox on your server to an external address, such as a Gmail account or your own.
2. Confirm the message arrives. In the received message headers you should see it passed through your mySMTP relay.
3. If the message does not arrive, check the server's mail queue or logs for delivery errors.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Login fails">
    Re-enter your mySMTP username and password in the smart host settings. Make sure there are no extra spaces.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Connection timed out">
    Check that outbound traffic on port **587** is allowed by your firewall. Try port **2525** if port 587 is blocked, and update the port in your server settings.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="TLS not available">
    If your server version does not support STARTTLS, confirm you are using a current release. hMailServer and MDaemon both support TLS on SMTP delivery in recent versions.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Relay access denied">
    Make sure the sender address uses a domain authenticated for your mySMTP account. See [DNS settings quick explainer](/getting-started/dns-settings-quick-explainer).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

For more error codes, see [Common SMTP errors](/troubleshooting/common-smtp-errors).
