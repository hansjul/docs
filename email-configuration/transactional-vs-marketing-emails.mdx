Separating these two streams protects your sender reputation and keeps critical messages out of the spam folder.

## What is a transactional email?

A transactional email is triggered by a specific user action or system event. It delivers information the recipient expects and needs.

### Common examples

| Type | Example subject |
| --- | --- |
| Order confirmation | Your order #12345 has been received |
| Password reset | Reset your password now |
| Account verification | Verify your email address |
| Shipping update | Your package is on the way |
| Payment receipt | Payment processed |

### Best practices for transactional email

- **Use a dedicated SMTP server or dedicated IP** so promotional sending does not delay password resets or receipts.
- **Send from a subdomain** such as `notifications.yourdomain.com` to isolate reputation.
- **Authenticate with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC** to prevent spoofing and improve trust with mailbox providers.
- **Deliver within seconds** of the trigger event. Use a high-performance relay with low latency.
- **Avoid promotional content** inside transactional messages. Mixing marketing language can cause spam foldering.

## What is a marketing email?

A marketing email promotes a product, service, or brand and is sent in bulk to a list. It requires explicit user consent under regulations such as GDPR, CAN-SPAM, and CCPA.

### Common examples

| Type | Example subject |
| --- | --- |
| Newsletter | February industry insights |
| Promotion | Save 20% on your next order |
| Product announcement | Introducing our new feature |
| Re-engagement | We miss you. Here is a special offer |
| Event invitation | Join our webinar on email security |

### Best practices for marketing email

- **Send from a separate subdomain** such as `marketing.yourdomain.com`.
- **Include a working unsubscribe link** in every message. For bulk senders, implement RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe headers.
- **Use confirmed opt-in** so only users who explicitly subscribe receive your emails.
- **Warm up your IP** before high-volume campaigns to build sender reputation gradually.
- **Monitor sender reputation** with Google Postmaster Tools and keep spam complaints below 0.3%.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Feature | Transactional emails | Marketing emails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Provide account or transaction updates | Promote products, services, or brand |
| Trigger | User action or system event | Scheduled campaign or automation |
| Opt-in required | No (implied by relationship) | Yes (explicit consent required) |
| Unsubscribe link | Optional | Mandatory in every message |
| Delivery speed | Immediate (within seconds) | Batched over minutes or hours |
| SMTP setup | Dedicated transactional relay | Bulk sending platform or marketing IP |
| Sender domain | `notifications.yourdomain.com` | `marketing.yourdomain.com` |
| Promotional content | Avoid | Expected and encouraged |

## Testing your authentication setup

Whether you send transactional or marketing emails, always verify your authentication before launching.

- Check SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records using the mySMTP [One-Click SPF / DKIM / DMARC Checker](https://toolbox.smtp.ai/).
- Run the [Sender Readiness Checklist](https://toolbox.smtp.ai/) to review authentication, rDNS, unsubscribe setup and list hygiene.
- Verify your HTML templates with the [Email Template Verifier](https://toolbox.smtp.ai/) before a major campaign.
- Send a test message to a Gmail and Outlook mailbox and inspect the headers.
